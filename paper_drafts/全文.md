\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) have transcended text utilities to become generative mirrors of the human mind, capable of replicating complex cognition. While utilizing these mirrors to simulate expert personas remains a major pursuit, LLM-based agents relying on profiles fail to mimic authentic reasoning under dilemmas due to surface-level alignment. To bridge this gap, we treat professional design as a testbed for non-linear cognition, thereby introducing a framework that operationalizes Mischel’s Cognitive-Affective Personality System theory as a cognitive engine. By mapping a 30‑dimensional trait layer into a PKVC state generator, this framework leverages behavioral data from 163 designers to encode empirical human trajectories into ontological procedures, dynamically modulating state transitions to guide the agent's reasoning along expert decision paths under high-conflict scenarios. Evaluations show that our framework significantly outperforms standard persona baselines, paving a novel methodology to formally decrypt, simulate, and scrutinize the underlying architecture of human expertise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\begin{quote}
\textit{``Language is not merely a tool for communication, but a mirror of the mind.'' \\—— Noam Chomsky}
\end{quote}

Simulating domain-specific expert personas has emerged as a fundamental pursuit for Large Language Model (LLM) based role-playing agents \cite{shanahan2023role}. However, when deployed to mimic authentic reasoning and decision-making under complex professional dilemmas, these agents consistently fail to produce high-fidelity behavioral trajectories \cite{du2026survey}. The core bottleneck is not a deficiency of factual knowledge. While LLMs excel at describing the static attributes of a domain, they lack the underlying computational cognitive structure to mirror how a human expert actually reasons through a crisis. Authentic decision-making under high-conflict dilemmas is inherently non-linear and dynamic, requiring a fluid shifting between redefining a problem, invoking past experiences, balancing competing values, and generating principles for action \cite{mischel1995cognitive}.

To bridge this gap, advanced agents deploy alignment techniques to mirror stable character profiles \cite{shao2023character}. However, these methods rely on static declarative information or post-hoc constraints to dictate what an agent should express. They fail to model how an expert navigates the non-linear, situated cognitive dynamics under conflict, nor can they externalize the rapidly shifting tacit knowledge embedded within professional choices. Consequently, reasoning chains \cite{zhang2025igniting} collapse into flat, general-purpose logic, missing the domain-specific state transitions that define true human expertise. We term this limitation ``surface-level persona alignment.''

Confronting these limitations, we pursue a paradigm shift toward \textbf{latent trait and epistemic state alignment} by treating professional design as an ideal testbed for non-linear cognition. Design reasoning represents a pinnacle of human problem-solving, where practitioners must continuously make high-stakes choices under wicked dilemmas. Under this testbed, we introduce a framework that operationalizes Walter Mischel’s seminal \textbf{Cognitive-Affective Personality System (CAPS) theory \cite{mischel1995cognitive} into a cognitive engine}. Our framework bridges latent traits of human experts with instantaneous reasoning states across two integrated layers: a 30-dimensional trait layer grounded in psychological constructs but localized to professional dilemmas, and a 4-state generator termed PKVC that formalizes the transitions of expert cognition: \textbf{Problem framing ($P$), Knowledge retrieval ($K$), Value judgment ($V$), and Concept generation ($C$)}.

The core innovation lies in the dynamic and computational interaction between these layers. Instead of treating the 30-dimensional expert traits as static text embeddings, we conceptualize them as behavioral disposition parameters that govern transitions across epistemic states. To ground this interaction empirically, we leverage behavioral tracking data from 163 professional designers facing high-conflict dilemmas to discover and extract formal ontological procedures that explicitly capture how these 30-dimensional psychological traits systematically guide the shifting between $P$, $K$, $V$ and $C$. At runtime, these ontological procedures dynamically parameterize and modulate the state-transition probabilities. Consequently, when the agent encounters a complex professional dilemma, its token generation is routed along authentic, non-linear expert paths, enforcing deep cognitive fidelity over surface-level imitation.

To our knowledge, this is the first framework to operationalize psychological CAPS theory to control the reasoning trajectories of LLM-based agents under complex dilemmas. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that our framework significantly outperforms standard persona baselines, achieving a high-fidelity simulation of expert decision-making. In a nutshell, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item We propose a cognitive agent framework that shifts LLM simulation from surface text alignment to latent trait and epistemic state alignment by formalizing a 30-dimensional trait layer and a 4-stage PKVC generator.
\item We establish an empirical paradigm grounded in behavioral data from 163 designers, transforming abstract psychological traits and states into executable ontological procedures that govern real-time reasoning transitions under high-conflict scenarios.
\item Evaluations show our framework outperforms standard persona baselines. More importantly, it offers bidirectional value: high-fidelity expert simulation for NLP community, and a computational tool for cognitive psychology to validate and scrutinize the generative architecture of human expertise.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Cognitive Modeling for LLM Agents}
Simulating consistent personas has evolved from early prompt-engineering methods \cite{han2022meet,shanahan2023role} to behavioral replication utilizing contrastive learning and reinforcement learning alignment \cite{ji2025enhancing,wang2025raiden,wang2025know}. To maintain long-term consistency against attention decay, recent approaches incorporate cognitive structures like Theory of Mind \cite{cross2025hypothetical,wen2026tracing} and self-reflection \cite{bai2026irote} to mimic internal mental states. Recent neuro-symbolic LLM agent systems inject structured domain constraints directly into LLM reasoning pipelines to enforce logical consistency \cite{banerjee2025crane,shi2025constraintllm,seo2026toward,labre2026ontology}. Nevertheless, these methods predominantly rely on static profiles or cognitive constraint validation to dictate \textit{what} an agent should express. They fundamentally lack execution-time adaptability and cannot model \textit{how} an expert dynamically navigates non-linear reasoning paths under conflict. 

\subsection{Simulation of Design Expertise}
From an epistemological perspective, design reasoning represents a pinnacle of human problem-solving \cite{lou2023human}, driven by situated, rapidly shifting tacit knowledge and cognition that is difficult to verbalize \cite{cross1982designerly,schindler2015expertise}. While prior knowledge engineering sought to externalize this process via static knowledge graphs \cite{jia2025knowledge}, recent LLM studies in professional design focus on deploying synthetic personas to simulate users or stakeholders for evaluation \cite{pang2025understanding,lin2025creativity,schmidt2024simulating,lu2025uxagent}. However, modeling the expert's internal, non-linear decision-making under high-conflict dilemmas remains unexplored. As an ideal computational testbed, professional design allows this work to formalize empirical expert trajectories, thereby aligning agent choices with authentic human expertise.

\section{Cognitive Agent Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
\subsection{Preliminaries}
In cognitive psychology, empirical data demonstrating that individuals are characterized not only by stable individual differences in their overall levels of behavior, but also by distinctive and stable patterns of behavior variability across situations \cite{mischel1995cognitive}. The core of our framework is grounded in cognitive CAPS theory, which posits that human behavior is not dictated by static traits alone, but emerges from a dynamic personality system interacting with specific situations. Instead of traditional ``if-then'' heuristic rules, CAPS conceptualizes personality as an interconnected network of cognitive-affective units that process situational features to generate behavioral outcomes.

To translate this psychological paradigm into an executable computational architecture for LLM-based agents, we must formally define the three core components dictated by CAPS before mapping them to specific functional modules:

\textbf{Situational Context ($\mathcal{C}$):} The input space representing the natural language description of a complex, high-conflict professional decision task or dilemma encountered by the agent.

\textbf{Latent Trait Layer ($\mathcal{T}$):} The expert abstraction and deep cognitive representation layer. It encapsulates a localized set of psychological and professional constructs that govern how an expert filters and evaluates the ongoing dilemma.

\textbf{Epistemic State Sequence ($\mathcal{E}_{PKVC}$):} The output sequence of the cognitive engine. Rather than generating flat text tokens directly, the agent outputs a sequence of discrete epistemic states $\mathcal{E}_{PKVC} = \{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_t\}$, where each state $e_i$ dynamically transitions within a cognitive 4-stage:

Grounded in these definitions, our framework operationalizes CAPS by introducing \textbf{ontological procedures} ($\mathcal{O}$) as guidance laws. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:framework}, these modules leverage empirical human trajectories to map the activated latent traits $\mathcal{T}$ under the situational context $\mathcal{C}$ into the real-time modulation of state-transition probabilities. Formally, the generation of the non-linear expert reasoning chain $\mathcal{E}_{PKVC}$ is defined as a controlled transition process, where the probability of entering the next epistemic state $e_t$ is conditioned on the prior state, inputs, and the ontological guidance:
\begin{equation}
P(e_t \mid e_{t-1}, \mathcal{C}, \mathcal{T}; \mathcal{O}).
\end{equation}
This probabilistic routing ensures that the agent's token generation follows authentic, domain-specific cognitive trajectories rather than generic textual associations.

\subsection{The Latent Trait Representation}
To computationalize the inner workings of CAPS, the latent trait layer $\mathcal{T}$ must transcend generic, domain-agnostic personality inventories. We therefore operationalize the stable cognitive-affective units within CAPS as \textbf{domain-specific professional dispositions} that capture how a practitioner encodes problems, tolerates ambiguity, evaluates risks, and prioritizes values under professional dilemmas. Instead of treating these traits as static descriptors, $\mathcal{T}$ acts as a conditional parameter space triggered by the situational context $\mathcal{C}$. \textbf{Under cases such as designing an Olympic stadium while facing tensions between technology and human-centeredness, global universality and local contextuality, or minimalism and organicity, different contextual conflicts may selectively activate different design traits. For some designers, traits such as Technological Acceleration may dominate, guiding cognition toward data-driven optimization, scalable systems, and technologically interventionist reasoning. For others, traits such as Local Cultural Adaptation may instead become activated, inhibiting abstract universal frameworks and reinforcing situated cultural specificity, contextual interpretation, and locally grounded design logic. (JIDAN GAIed)}. To guarantee psychometric validity and discriminative power, this deep cognitive representation layer is derived via a three-fold elicitation paradigm:

(1) \textbf{Contextual Transduction:} Translating classical psychological constructs into localized professional design conflicts.

(2) \textbf{Decontextualized Probing:} Utilizing abstract declarative statements to minimize immediate task-specific priming biases.

(3) \textbf{Polarized Forcing:} Employing forced-choice trade-off formats to maximize statistical variance and discriminability.

Ultimately, this systematic elicitation yields a $30$-dimensional trait vector $\mathbf{t} \in \mathbb{R}^{30}$ for each practitioner. This resulting vector $\mathbf{t}$ serves as the precise behavioral profile that dynamically parameterizes the downstream epistemic state transitions.

\subsection{State Generator and Reasoning}
The core operational innovation of our framework lies in establishing the computational linkage between the latent trait vector $\mathbf{t}$ and the active state transitions via CAPS, permitting highly non-linear behavioral trajectories, including iterative loops, backward revisions, and opportunistic selective bypassing among states. Specifically, we operationalize individual traits not as static text prompts, but as dynamic parameters that modulate the conditional state transitions. To render these transition paths actionable for LLM reasoning control, we inject the latent trait vector $\mathbf{t}$, the situational context $\mathcal{C}$, and empirical ontological procedures directly into the language model's context window. Under this paradigm, the LLM performs explicit state inference, where the agent's cognition is generated as a structured, chronological sequence of discrete epistemic states, and their transitions trace the expert's non-linear thinking paths.

To formally parameterize and constrain this state inference process, we compile the empirical distributions derived from expert behavior into formalized \textbf{Dynamic Ontological Triplets} to construct a context-aware cognitive routing mechanism, defined as:
\begin{equation}\mathcal{O}{trip} = (\mathcal{C}{type}, \mathbf{t}{sub}, p, e{target})
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_{type}$ denotes the structural taxonomy of the situational context, $\mathbf{t}_{sub} \subset \mathbf{t}$ represents a salient activated trait sub-vector, $e_{target} \in \mathcal{E}_{PKVC}$ is the targeted epistemic destination state within the thought tree, and $p$ represents a functional cognitive routing predicate (e.g.,\texttt{forces\_transition\_to}, \texttt{severely\_suppresses}, \texttt{micro\_manipulates\_within})) that establishes the valid structural edges between thought nodes.

\section{Empirical Trait-to-State Alignment and Ontological Runtime Engine}

\subsection{Dual-Component Empirical Tracking}
We recruited and screened 200 professional designers to participate in this study. Participants were required to complete a two-part assessment with a minimum time interval of half an hour between the sessions. Prior to the test, participants provided design-related background information (Personal Identity), which served as part of the baseline data for subsequent experiments. 

The first half of the assessment consisted of 30 questions measured on a 6-point Likert scale regarding latent trait tendencies. This exposed the professional designers to standardized opposing dilemmas to capture their true underlying characteristics. The second half revolved around two complex design tasks (Task 1 included single-choice and ranking questions, while Task 2 involved open-ended questions), which were utilized to train and validate their design reasoning processes. 

During the empirical data collection for Design Task 1, we strictly decoupled the trait stimulus space from the behavioral state space. Specifically, the task context was constructed as dense semantic text capable of simultaneously triggering multiple latent dimensions, while the response options explicitly excluded trait-related keywords, serving purely as representations of action state transitions.  Ultimately, through rigorous quality screening, we obtained 163 valid responses.

\subsection{Relationship Mining and Robustness Analysis}

Through a statistical analysis framework utilizing Multi-layer Cross-validation, we verified the statistical significance of the ``trait-state'' patterns and ensured the robustness of our conclusions across three core dimensions: 

First, based on latent variables extracted via Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA), we employed Permutation Tests to explore the significant associations between traits and behaviors. Second, we introduced Multinomial LASSO regression (with an L1 penalty) for fine-grained analysis at the scale-item level, effectively controlling the false positive expectations caused by multiple comparisons in a high-dimensional space. Finally, through 1,000 Bootstrap resampling iterations and partialing out demographic covariates, we confirmed the high robustness of the model signals.

Concurrently, during the clustering exploration of the data structure (utilizing GMM/K-means coupled with t-SNE visualization), this study achieved a critical methodological breakthrough: the empirical data demonstrated that designer traits manifest as a continuous manifold distribution (Trait-continuous), rather than discrete predefined archetypes.

\subsection{Statistical Data-Driven Construction of Behavioral Ontology Quadruples}

Our system formalizes the behavioral logic of designers into ontological quad-relations (detailed in Section 3). In traditional ontology engineering, rule extraction typically relies on manually defined ``Competency Questions (CQs)''. However, when mapping complex psychological traits to state decisions, the a priori intuition of human experts is often prone to subjective bias. Consequently, this study abandons the traditional qualitative CQ-driven paradigm in favor of an ontology construction approach driven entirely by statistical empirical data mapping.

The core innovation lies in the automated derivation of the ``routing predicate'' within the quad-relation, generated directly from multinomial LASSO regression results using rigorous statistical thresholds. The specific mapping rules are as follows:

First, the system implements strict noise filtering, retaining only trait features with a marginal significance of $p < 0.10$ for inclusion in the ontology repository. Subsequently, fine-grained semantic predicate mapping is executed based on the effect size ($|\text{coef}|$) and direction (positive or negative correlation):

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strongly forces transition To:} Triggered by a highly significant ($p < 0.05$) positive correlation with an extreme effect size ($|\text{coef}| \ge 0.50$). This grants the trait absolute dominance; the Large Language Model (LLM) bypasses trade-off considerations during Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and directly transitions to the target state.
    
    \item \textbf{Guides transition to):} Triggered by a significant ($p < 0.05$) positive correlation with a moderate effect size ($0.20 \le |\text{coef}| < 0.50$), serving as the primary directional heuristic for the LLM's reasoning.
    
    \item \textbf{Weakly shifts to:} Applied to marginally significant ($0.05 \le p < 0.10$) positive correlations, functioning as a fallback or compromise trajectory in the absence of stronger constraints.
    
    \item \textbf{Strictly inhibits:} Triggered by a negative correlation coefficient ($-$) with an effect size of $|\text{coef}| \ge 0.20$. This establishes a ``repulsive force'' rule at the ontological level, compelling the LLM to explicitly articulate a rejection of this state during its logical generation.
\end{itemize}

Through the aforementioned Numeric-to-Semantic compilation rules, this study successfully and losslessly transforms abstract statistical data into structured knowledge rules directly interpretable by LLMs, yielding a total of 36 ontology quad-relations (14 positive and 22 negative). This provides an immutable, mathematically and empirically validated foundation to regulate the CoT reasoning chain for subsequent LLM role-playing.

\subsection{Prompt-Guided State Routing}

The 36 quad-relations constitute a static ontology repository. This subsection describes how a subset is activated per agent at inference time and injected into the prompt to constrain generation at the token level.

For each agent, we filter the ontology against the agent's 30-dimensional Likert profile, retaining only those quads whose required trait direction agrees with the agent's response polarity. We then sample this filtered set in a PKVC-stratified manner---keeping the highest-$|\beta|$ quad within each of the four epistemic states and adding one overall maximum---to obtain an activated set $\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{t})$ of four to five quads. Stratification is indispensable here: a naive top-$k$ cut on $|\beta|$ would let the empirically dominant $V$ state crowd out signals from the remaining three.

Each quad's natural-language routing description is pre-compiled offline during ontology construction and persisted as a JSON field; at inference, descriptions are retrieved verbatim and concatenated into a routing block, preceded by a four-line $\beta$ summary aggregating the signed activation of each PKVC state to provide a state-level numeric anchor. The routing block is embedded in a composite prompt that further specifies (i) a per-segment JSON schema constraining \texttt{state\_sequence} and \texttt{nl\_answer}; (ii) the agent's demographic profile and Likert-strong stance labels as static priors; (iii) the agent's Q3--Q6 conflict-resolution behaviors as trajectory exemplars; and (iv) for boundary-judgment segments, a deterministic rule mapping the strongest positive-$\beta$ state to the corresponding bottom-line anchor. We denote this configuration as $\textbf{v2\_anchor}$ and report cross-model generalization and per-component ablations in Section~5.

\section{Experiments and Evaluation}
\subsection{Evaluation Benchmark}
We evaluate the proposed framework on a designer-centered role-play benchmark grounded in real behavioral data from 162 designers. The benchmark adopts a cross-scenario transfer setting: models are conditioned on participants' SADR and PKVC from Q1--Q6, and are then evaluated on an unseen dilemma scenario Q7. Q7 is divided into three evaluation segments corresponding to different stages of expert cognition:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Seg1}: immediate intuitive reaction;
    \item \textbf{Seg2}: decision-weighing reasoning chain;
    \item \textbf{Seg3}: bottom-line and compromise reasoning.
\end{itemize}

Each segment is independently generated using stateless prompting rather than autoregressive continuation, ensuring strict parity between training and evaluation schemas.

Overall, the benchmark contains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item 162 designers;
    \item 3 evaluation segments per designer;
    \item 486 designer-segment evaluation instances.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Model Configuration}
All role-play inference experiments are evaluated across five diverse Large Language Models (LLMs) to verify cross-model generalizability, including \textbf{GPT-4o, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite , Claude Haiku 4.5, DeepSeek V4 Flash, and Qwen 3.6 35B A3B}.For all role-play inference, the generation temperature is strictly set to 0.7 with a maximum generation length of 600 tokens. PKVC sequence extraction is universally executed using GPT-4o under deterministic decoding ($temperature = 0$) with a constrained JSON schema.
For semantic similarity and persona consistency evaluation, we use \textbf{text-embedding-3-small} to obtain 1536-dimensional sentence embeddings.

\subsection{Compared Configurations}
\subsubsection{baseline models}
we construct 5 progressive baseline settings:
\paragraph{B1: Zero-shot}
The model receives only the target dilemma scenario and task instruction, without any designer-specific persona information.

\paragraph{B2: Zero-shot + Personal Identity (PI)}
Building upon B1, this setting additionally injects static personal identity information, including design field, years of experience, educational background, project role, working context.

\paragraph{B3: PI+SADR Persona + CoT}
This baseline further introduces dynamic cognitive stance labels derived from the CAPS-based trait framework, together with explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning instructions.

\paragraph{B4: PI+SADR Persona + RAG}
Building upon B3, this setting further retrieves natural-language responses from top-$3$ trait-similar peer designers as additional context.The retrieved responses originate from Q1--Q6, and serve as stylistic and cognitive references during generation.
\subsubsection{The SOTA Configuration: v2\_anchor}
Representing our finalized optimal design, this setting implements a structured system prompt containing four-class epistemic state definitions, strict output JSON schemas, and an explicit numerical decision-making rule for seg3 in Q7. The user prompt integrated PI, SADR Persona, historical conflict-resolution behaviors from Q3--Q6 and a CAPS quadruples block.
\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
We evaluate role-play quality from 6 complementary perspectives: PKVC dominant accuracy, syntactic similarity, semantic similarity, pragmatic similarity, PKVC path fidelity, and persona consistency.

\subsection{Experimental Results}

\subsection{Ablation Analysis}
To analyze the contribution of different information sources, we conduct modular ablation experiments.We execute 6 fine-grained ablation variants derived from the \textbf{v2\_anchor} configuration. All variants are cross-evaluated across the five multi-scale language models under identical hyperparameter settings.
\paragraph{v2\_no\_caps (Without CAPS Module)}
An ablation variant building upon \textbf{v2\_anchor} where the entire CAPS ontological block---including both the quick-glance numeric header and the detailed trajectory-to-state routing descriptions---is completely discarded, isolating the direct necessity of empirical cognitive alignment.

\paragraph{v2\_no\_seg3\_rule (Without Seg3 Decision Rule)}
An ablation variant building upon \textbf{v2\_anchor} where the explicit quantitative rule for boundary decision-making is removed from the system prompt, evaluating the model's capacity to internalize and execute implicit numeric signals without hardcoded semantic constraints.

\paragraph{v2\_no\_beta\_summary (Without $\beta$ Summary Header)}
An ablation variant building upon \textbf{v2\_anchor} where only the quick-glance numeric intensity header within the CAPS block is stripped while retaining the dense natural-language routing descriptions, testing the efficiency of simplified numeric cheat-sheets.

\paragraph{v2\_no\_pkvc\_def (Without PKVC definitions)}
An ablation variant building upon \texttt{v2\_anchor} where the architectural definitions of the four fundamental epistemic states are completely removed from the premise of the system prompt, evaluating the baseline domain-knowledge parsing capability of the models.

\paragraph{v2\_plus\_peer (With Peer Block Augmentation)}
An augmented variant building upon \texttt{v2\_anchor} that incorporates peer-retrieval mechanisms, appending an additional peer block containing the natural-language responses of the top-3 trait-similar peer designers before the output instructions to explore the interaction between raw text references and statistical ontological constraints.

\paragraph{v2\_hybrid (With Per-Segment Dispatching)}
A mixed ablation variant that deploys a per-segment prompt dispatching strategy, utilizing the complete \textbf{v2\_anchor} for segments 1 and 2, while degrading segment 3 to an unstructured condition by removing the quantitative decision rule and changing the CAPS block from four-class balanced filtering to strength-based top-k ranking.
\section{Conclusion}

We introduced a cognitive agent framework that operationalizes Mischel's CAPS theory as a runtime routing engine for LLM-based expert role-play. Rather than relying on static persona descriptors, our framework grounds the latent trait layer in a 30-dimensional designer-specific construct space and compiles empirical trait-to-state mappings into 36 ontological quad-relations that constrain token-level generation through a structured prompt block (the v2\_anchor configuration). On the designer Q7 benchmark, our configuration uniformly outperforms the four-tier zero-shot-to-behavior baseline ladder across all five evaluation metrics on GPT-4o, with non-overlapping 95\% confidence intervals on macro-TED, non-V recall, chain diversity, and criterion uniqueness. The improvement transfers across five LLMs spanning four providers and three scales, with 24 of 25 (model, metric) cells moving in the favorable direction (96\% agreement). Component ablation further confirms that the CAPS routing block is the load-bearing element: removing it inflates macro-TED by 32\% and flips trait-prediction Spearman $\rho$ from positive to negative, the most consistent ablation signal in our metric suite. These findings suggest a viable path beyond surface-level persona alignment toward agents whose internal trajectories are governed by computational instantiations of cognitive theory, and offer a bidirectional contribution: a high-fidelity expert simulation paradigm for the NLP community and a computational instrument for cognitive psychology to scrutinize the generative architecture of human expertise.

\section{Limitation}

Several limitations of this work merit explicit discussion. \textbf{First}, our evaluation is grounded in a single expert domain (professional design) and a single high-conflict scenario (Q7), so generalization to other expertise areas and dilemma structures remains untested. \textbf{Second}, the sample of 162 valid designers is recruited from a single regional population, limiting demographic and cultural diversity. \textbf{Third}, while the proposed prompt design generalizes across five LLMs, the smallest model in our cross-model suite (Qwen 3.6 27B) exhibited severe degradation on most v2-family conditions and was excluded from the cross-model analysis; this suggests that the proposed routing block requires a minimum instruction-following capacity that lighter or older backbones may not satisfy. \textbf{Fourth}, although trait-prediction Spearman $\rho$ shows the most consistent CAPS-attributable effect across models, its absolute magnitude remains modest (point estimates in $[+0.030, +0.172]$ across models) and only the peer-augmented variant attains a confidence interval fully above zero; stronger evidence will require either larger samples or richer trait elicitation. \textbf{Fifth}, surface-level NL specificity remains essentially flat ($\approx 1.0$) across all conditions and models, indicating that our prompt design improves cognitive routing without changing verbal style — a separation that is theoretically desirable but means our framework does not yet address surface-level persona mimicry. \textbf{Sixth}, peer retrieval presents an unresolved trade-off: it produces the only configuration with a strictly positive trait-prediction $\rho$ interval, but at a $41\%$ cost on macro-TED, and we have not identified a way to reap its personalization gains without the corresponding fidelity loss. \textbf{Seventh}, our PKVC sequence extractor is implemented as a constrained-decoding pass through GPT-4o; while pair-wise inter-extractor agreement remains acceptable, using a frontier model as both an inference backend (one of five) and as the universal evaluator introduces a potential self-consistency bias that future work should mitigate via cross-model evaluation. \textbf{Finally}, we report uncorrected 95\% bootstrap intervals across five metrics organized along three orthogonal dimensions, deliberately avoiding Bonferroni correction; consequently, individual metric-level claims should be read as informative rather than family-wise significant.
